# IRIS Theory

## Information, Replication, Invariance and Spacetime

**Author:** Misael Moreno
**Initial publication:** 2026
**Status:** Theoretical and exploratory framework

---

## Abstract

The **IRIS Theory** proposes a theoretical framework for exploring relationships between mass-energy equivalence, information, quantum correlations, spacetime, and the possibility of controlled state replication across different spatial and temporal coordinates.

The theory introduces a corrective factor intended to represent the physical and informational conditions required for a theoretical transformation or replication process.

The initial formulation is expressed as:

[
E = m c^2 \kappa \psi(T,L)
]

where:

* (E) represents the associated energy.
* (m) represents mass.
* (c) represents the speed of light.
* (\kappa) represents a correction or scaling coefficient.
* (\psi(T,L)) represents a function dependent on temporal and spatial conditions.
* (T) represents time.
* (L) represents spatial location.

The purpose of this repository is to make the theoretical framework transparent, reproducible, critically accessible, and available for independent analysis.

---

## Scientific Status

IRIS is currently a **theoretical hypothesis/framework**.

The equations and concepts presented in this repository should not be interpreted as experimentally validated physical laws.

The purpose of the project is to:

1. Formalize the mathematical framework.
2. Identify its assumptions.
3. Determine whether the proposed relationships are mathematically consistent.
4. Compare the framework with established physical theories.
5. Identify predictions that could, in principle, be experimentally tested.
6. Develop computational models and simulations.
7. Encourage independent scientific criticism and replication of proposed calculations.

No claim of experimentally demonstrated matter replication, time travel, teleportation, or violation of established physical laws is made by this repository.

# Core Concept

IRIS explores whether the state of a physical system can be represented through a sufficiently complete combination of:

* Matter
* Energy
* Information
* Spatial coordinates
* Temporal coordinates
* Quantum state
* Environmental conditions

The central question is:

> Can a physically meaningful state be mathematically described in a way that permits its reconstruction or controlled transformation at another spacetime coordinate while remaining consistent with conservation laws and quantum mechanics?

This question is treated as a theoretical research problem rather than an established physical capability.

---

# Initial Mathematical Formulation

The initial IRIS formulation is:

[
E = m c^2 \kappa \psi(T,L)
]

The simplest initial condition is:

[
\kappa = 1
]

and:

[
\psi(T,L) = 1
]

Under these conditions, the formulation reduces to the familiar mass-energy relationship:

[
E = mc^2
]

This provides a baseline from which additional mathematical terms can be investigated.

# The Correction Factor

The parameter (\kappa) is introduced as a theoretical correction factor.

A future formulation may investigate whether (\kappa) can represent quantities such as:

* Information requirements
* Quantum-state fidelity
* Environmental interaction
* Energy losses
* Entropy
* Measurement constraints
* Spatial displacement
* Temporal displacement
* Decoherence
* Gravitational effects
* Other physical constraints

The exact mathematical interpretation of (\kappa) remains an open research problem.

---

# The Spacetime Function

The function:

[
\psi(T,L)
]

is intended to represent the dependence of the proposed process on temporal and spatial coordinates.

An important objective of the project is to determine whether such a function can be mathematically defined using quantities that have established physical meaning.

Possible future variables include:

[
\psi = \psi(T,x,y,z,\rho,S,I,\ldots)
]

where variables could represent time, position, density, entropy, information, or other measurable quantities.

These variables are hypotheses for investigation and are not asserted to be the final formulation.

# Research Objectives

The initial research program includes:

### 1. Mathematical consistency

Determine whether the equations can be formulated without internal contradictions.

### 2. Dimensional analysis

Every proposed equation must satisfy dimensional consistency.

For example:

[
[E] = [mc^2]
]

Therefore, any correction terms introduced into the equation must be dimensionally justified.

### 3. Conservation laws

Investigate compatibility with:

* Conservation of energy
* Conservation of momentum
* Conservation of angular momentum
* Charge conservation
* Other applicable conservation laws

### 4. Quantum mechanics

Investigate the relationship between IRIS and established concepts including:

* Quantum states
* Entanglement
* Measurement
* Decoherence
* Quantum information
* No-cloning theorem

### 5. Relativity

Investigate compatibility with:

* Special relativity
* General relativity
* Spacetime geometry
* Causality
* Relativistic energy and momentum

### 6. Information theory

Investigate whether information can be incorporated into the model in a physically meaningful and measurable manner.

### 7. Computational simulation

Develop simulations capable of testing simplified versions of the mathematical model.

### 8. Experimental proposals

Identify predictions that could potentially distinguish the IRIS framework from existing theories.

# Falsifiability

A central principle of this project is falsifiability.

IRIS should not be considered successful merely because its equations can be mathematically constructed.

A meaningful theory must produce consequences that can potentially be shown to be false.

Future development should therefore prioritize identifying:

1. Quantitative predictions.
2. Boundary conditions.
3. Measurable parameters.
4. Expected experimental outcomes.
5. Results that would invalidate specific parts of the model.
